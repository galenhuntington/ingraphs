# Vertex-region SAT repair and solver reuse, 2026-10-03

## Outcome

No new refuters or improvements to the recorded seed-pool minima. Survivor
lists remain 25 / 61 / 31 at n=14 / 15 / 16; those lists and proof scripts were
not changed.

There is a stronger local exclusion now: **all 9,043 distinct two-vertex
rewiring regions from the selected seeds, across all 117 candidates, were
UNSAT**, after replaying 15 short timeouts. This allows any combination of
changes on edges incident to two vertices, not merely two edge flips. It rules
out refuters whose changes from one of those seeds are supported on at most
two vertices. It says nothing about other seeds or changes spread over more
vertices, and these are native solver results, not independently checked
UNSAT proof certificates.

For F=`208069916672`, all **39 three-vertex region classes** of seed
`225217471804806150894596317` were also exhausted, including four replays.
The two-copy n=14 target remains unrefuted; only part of its much larger
three-vertex catalogue was examined.

Fresh solvers were better than retained state in a small matched-region
comparison. Four-vertex blocks and core-guided expansion did not produce a
frontier success. The useful implementation advance is systematic, deduplicated
vertex rewiring, not a demonstrated breakthrough in finding refuters.

## New implementation

`graphy ingraph-sat` and `scripts/sat_repair.py` now accept:

```
--block-mode vertex --vertices R [--max-vertices M]
```

For selected vertices S, every edge incident to S is free, including edges
within S. Only the induced graph H[V-S] is fixed. The number of free variables
is choose(n,2)-choose(n-R,2):

| R | n=14 | n=15 | n=16 |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2 | 25 | 27 | 29 |
| 3 | 36 | 39 | 42 |
| 4 | 46 | 50 | 54 |

`--block-size` is ignored in this mode. `--free-edges` still overrides the
selector, but cannot be combined with `--max-vertices`.

### Exact catalogue deduplication

If two fixed induced graphs are isomorphic, a bijection between their fixed
vertices can be extended to the remaining vertices. It maps completions of
one region to completions of the other, preserving monochromatic-copy absence.
Complementing the entire colouring also preserves the property. Therefore
only one representative per fixed-graph isomorphism/complement class is needed
for **feasibility**, even across different seeds. This does not enumerate all
labelled refuters and does not make a timeout into an exclusion.

The selector enumerates all R-subsets of the input seed pool, retains original
host labels, deduplicates using exact internal canonical keys, then shuffles
the distinct classes. The catalogue is cheap in these experiments (typically
milliseconds). No live SAT clause is relabelled or simplified under old fixed
values. Default non-vertex modes are unchanged.

For example, the six-copy n=14 candidate `208069916672` with its selected seed
has 39 classes from 364 three-vertex subsets, and 88 from 1,001 four-vertex
subsets. These counts are **not** for the two-copy target `208095050752`, whose
two selected seeds give 590 and 1,378 classes respectively.

Each class is attempted at most once per run, even if unresolved. `--blocks`
is an upper bound, not a promise of that many rows. Additional independent
restarts revisit classes rather than partitioning the catalogue. Without an
input seed, vertex mode builds its catalogue from one random seed.

### Optional expansion

After a genuine local UNSAT, `--max-vertices M` permits freeing one additional
vertex. Vertices touching more core edges are preferred, with random ties.
The child keeps its anchor and labels; the released edges strictly include
the parent's. Only one expansion branch is followed. Timeouts and resource
limits do not trigger expansion. A full core gives random tie-breaking, not
substantive guidance. Core minimization was not implemented.

Children consume the same overall `--blocks` allowance, get their own
`--seconds` budget, and normally retain solver learning. `--reset-every 1`
also resets between parent and child; their logical regions remain nested.
All attempted classes are deduplicated with their remaining order included
in the key. `--relabel` applies at chain roots, not between parent and child.

CSV adds `free_vertices`, `fixed_core`, and `parent_block`. The core key is an
internal canonical representation at order n-popcount(free_vertices), not the
least-decimal form or a stable cross-version identifier. A small status fix
also recognizes an **empty** UNSAT assumption core as
`unrestricted-unsat-unverified`, even when the query had fixed edges. It does
not produce a checked global proof.

### Solver reuse

Six timed-out three-vertex regions were replayed from scratch at root
`2026100304`, 30 seconds, 100,000 rounds, same anchors/free masks, one block
per process. All were UNSAT: three n=14 cases in 0.53–0.80 seconds; two n=15
cases in 1.18 and 16.56 seconds; one n=16 case in 4.41 seconds.
Files: `sat14-20261003-replay-{a,b,c}`, `sat15-20261003-replay-{a,b}`,
`sat16-20261003-replay-a`. These were fresh searches, not resumed solver states.

The follow-up `sat14-20261003-v3-{warm,cold}` used root `2026100305`, the
same three n=14 targets, 12 blocks each, three seconds/block, three jobs.
All 36 anchor/free-mask/fixed-core triples were checked to be identical.

| State policy | UNSAT | Timeout | Summed seconds |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| retain (`--reset-every 0`) | 31 | 5 | 45.349 |
| fresh (`--reset-every 1`) | 36 | 0 | 25.634 |

This is evidence for trying frequent resets, not proof that retaining clauses
is always worse. Resetting also changes the native solver's seed to walk_seed
plus block index, and workload overlap was not identical. Defaults were not
changed. Within an expanding chain, retained clauses may still help.

## Controls and validation

The three known refuters from the October 2 note were damaged by complementing
every edge incident to vertices 0 or 1 (25/27/29 edge changes). The selector
was **not told** which vertices were damaged.

`sat-control{14,15,16}-20261003-v2` used vertices=2, blocks=100,
seconds=0.5, batch=8, root `2026100303`. It recovered the known refuter classes
at blocks 23, 80 and 6, after 0.057, 0.179 and 0.473 summed seconds. Returned
distances were 13, 13 and 29: two found equivalent labelled graphs rather than
literally undoing every damaged edge. Independent Python checks passed;
canonical/complement keys matched the known classes. Cross-checking against
all current survivors gave no additional refutations.

Conversely, `sat-control{14,15,16}-20261003-grow1-3`, starting at one vertex
and allowing expansion to three, found nothing within 32 attempts (27 for
n=16 after its initial catalogue ended). Greedy core expansion can miss a
feasible region; it is not a complete traversal of larger vertex sets.

Tests include exhaustive small completion comparisons for deduplicated
regions, exact star masks, core-guided expansion, maintained labels under
relabelling/resetting, interruption/no-expansion behaviour, and independent
enumeration of dumped CNFs including empty-core global UNSAT.

All 47 release Rust tests and all 32 Python tests passed. The ten SAT-specific
Rust tests also passed with `--no-default-features --features sat,u64`, including
the pure-Rust canonicalizer. Across all frontier experiments there were 9,570
block attempts (including repeated regions/replays), 9,326 local UNSATs and
244 timeouts, totalling about 1,046 summed block wall seconds. Timeout rows
remain in the original files even where a later replay resolved that region.

## Build environment

The initial comparisons used the same Nix-shell release build on both sides.
Its wrapper warned that `NIX_ENFORCE_NO_NATIVE` suppressed `-march=native`.
After the Home Manager update, the already-running tool environment still had
no `LIBCLANG_PATH`; plain Cargo found Debian libclang and failed loading its
`libstdc++`. Pointing `LIBCLANG_PATH` at the available Nix libclang fixed the
build without a Nix shell or `NIX_ENFORCE_NO_NATIVE`. No profile was changed.
The final build uses `native,sat`; no extra CXXFLAGS were supplied.

## Suggested next campaign

Do not repeat the completed two-vertex scans with the same seed pool. For
another bounded campaign, sample three-vertex regions across **all n=14
candidates**, with fresh solvers, rather than making already expensive
four-vertex regions larger:

```sh
python3 scripts/sat_repair.py research/repair14-seeds-2026-09-28.csv \
    --candidates output/batches/all14 --keep 2 --restarts 1 --jobs 5 \
    --block-mode vertex --vertices 3 --reset-every 1 \
    --blocks 64 --seconds 5 --batch 8 --max-constraints 500000 \
    --rng-seed 2026100310 \
    > output/sat14-vertex3-wide.csv 2> output/sat14-vertex3-wide.log
```

At most 1,600 attempts / 8,000 summed budget seconds: roughly 27 minutes of
budgeted work on five workers, plus setup/soft-limit overhead, likely shorter
when regions exhaust early. It examines new candidates/regions, not a guaranteed
disjoint partition of previous samples. `--blocks 10000` instead walks the
complete finite catalogue, but should not be mistaken for this short budget.
More restarts revisit classes; more seed classes or a larger vertex count
actually enlarge the union of permitted regions.

I would leave expansion off for this comparison: it worked as specified but
did not outperform direct catalogue sampling in these trials. Remaining ideas
include minimizing useful assumption cores and symmetry-breaking among the
fully free vertices. Neither is implemented or benchmarked here.
