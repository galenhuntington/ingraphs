# Binary-translation / Klein-four orbital SAT

## Scope

The new `--preset klein` completes the proposed Klein-four catalog. The
broader `--preset binary` adds a deliberately bounded higher-rank portfolio.
These are generators of pair partitions for the existing SAT engine, **not**
enumerations of all their graph colourings. No Rust changes or rebuild are
needed if the existing binary has SAT support and sufficient graph width.

Files:

- `scripts/binary_families.py`: binary actions, action-equivalence pruning,
  and an independent algebraic pair-orbit count.
- `scripts/orbital_families.py`: `xor:...`, the `v4:...` shorthand, exceptions,
  and preset integration.
- `scripts/orbital_sat.py`: the new preset names; unchanged task identities,
  ledger schema, SAT backend, and retry/terminal-state rules.
- `scripts/test_binary_families.py`, `test_orbital_sat.py`,
  `test_capabilities.py`: action completeness, partition correctness,
  independent small-family search, ledger integration, and wide-graph tests.

No existing campaign ledger or survivor file is modified by the experiments
below. Their fresh ledger is `output/orbital-binary14-20261101.sqlite`.

## What the actions mean

Take a global group G = F₂ʳ. A transitive vertex block is a quotient G/H.
It is specified here by a basis of its character space W = H-perp, encoded
as small binary row masks. If W has d independent rows, the block has 2^d
vertices, labelled by local binary coordinates. Global generator j toggles
the local coordinate corresponding to each row whose j-th bit is set.
All blocks use the **same** global generators. Remaining vertices are fixed
individually; their graph degrees are unconstrained, not forced to zero.

For example, `xor:1.2.4+1.2+1` has blocks of 8, 4, and 2 vertices. The rows
1, 2, and 4 select three global bits a, b, c; row 3 would select a XOR b.
Dots separate independent rows within a block; plus signs separate blocks.
Explicit templates support masks 1..15 (up to four global bits), any number
of blocks fitting the host order, and the usual `/fixed` or `/0+4` exceptions.
Dependent rows *within one block* are rejected. Shared rows across blocks
are intentional: they couple the actions on those blocks.

`v4:3` is shorthand for `xor:1.2+1.2+1.2`. At n=14 it has 28 pair-orbits,
although each of its three nonidentity permutations separately has 49.
A low-variable noncyclic family can therefore represent graphs missed by
a cyclic search with a low pair-orbit cap. This does **not** say the graph
families are disjoint: some colourings have additional symmetry and occur
in other searches too.

The variable count has an independent formula:

- Within a block W: |W|-1 pair-orbits, one per nonzero XOR difference.
- Between blocks W and U: |W intersect U| pair-orbits.
- A fixed vertex is a separate block with character space {0}.

The generator computes actual pair partitions by orbit traversal. Tests
compare them to this formula and to explicit full-group closure/Burnside
counts, including masks above bit 127.

### Exact coverage and deduplication

Every faithful Klein-four action is a multiset of regular 4-vertex blocks,
three possible kinds of 2-vertex block, and fixed vertices. Changes of global
basis permute those three kinds arbitrarily. Enumerating these multisets,
modulo those six basis changes, is complete up to permutation-group/vertex
relabelling. Nonfaithful actions reduce to a cyclic group or the identity
and are deliberately not part of `klein`.

`binary` is the union of:

1. The complete faithful rank-two catalog (`klein`).
2. Every faithful rank-three action with **at most four non-singleton vertex
   blocks**, modulo all 168 invertible changes of global basis.
3. Faithful rank-four actions with **at most four non-singleton blocks**,
   each a coordinate projection. Only permutations of the four global
   coordinates are used for this equivalence pruning. This is a bounded
   subclass of rank-four actions, not a completeness claim.

Presets add no exception releases automatically. Exact equal partitions are
deduplicated by the existing launcher. Different group actions can still
have overlapping graph families; higher-rank pruning is not a claim of
graph-isomorphism-free enumeration. Variables are filtered **after** building
each partition, so explicit exception templates obey the same variable cap.

Catalog sizes, before any task exclusions:

| n | Klein <=30 | Klein <=35 | Binary <=25 | Binary <=30 | Binary <=35 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 14 | 6 | 18 | 50 | 71 | 91 |
| 15 | 0 | 4 | 28 | 44 | 65 |
| 16 | 0 | 0 | 35 | 62 | 77 |
| 17 | 0 | 0 | 9 | 38 | 58 |

The n=17 augmented-cube refuter from October 27 lies in the 26-variable
family `xor:1.2+4.8+1.4+2.8`. The four blocks are A₀, A₁, B₀, B₁ in that
note's construction, plus the fixed apex. Independent translations of the
four bits t₀₀, t₀₁, t₁₀, t₁₁ preserve the cross-edge conditions a_j=b_i.
The preset contains an equivalent action, possibly with different vertex
labels. This is an inclusion check, not a claim of a newly found refutation.

## Local experiments and verification

All experiments use the current SAT-enabled u128 executable, the 24 current
n=14 candidates, four workers, batch 64, and the usual 100,000-round /
500,000-constraint caps. The time budget is **per candidate/family task**.

| Campaign | Tasks | Seconds/task | Family UNSAT | Time limit | Wall seconds |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Klein, <=35 variables | 432 | 3 | 1 | 431 | 328.9 |
| Binary, <=25 variables | 1,200 | 2 | 1,171 | 29 | 98.6 |
| Retry those 29 binary tasks | 29 | 10 | 18 | 11 | 56.3 |
| Retry the three 28-variable Klein families | 72 | 10 | 0 | 72 | 182.0 |

No new refutations occurred in these campaigns. After the binary retry,
**49 of its 50 families are excluded for all 24 candidates**. The remaining
eleven tasks all use `xor:2.4+2.4+2.4+1` (25 variables): a regular Klein-four
action on three four-vertex blocks plus an independently swapped two-vertex
block. Those eleven are reasonable cheap follow-ups at 30 seconds. The much
larger unresolved Klein-four set still warrants a deliberately bounded
increase in budget: all three 28-variable families remained unresolved even
at ten seconds. These trials do not provide evidence of a refuter, but neither
do they exclude this family space. The proposed 120-second run is an experiment,
not a runtime estimate for completing those tasks.

Artifacts (all ignored by Git, with committed outcomes also in the ledger):

- `output/orbital14-klein-20261101-pilot.csv` and `.log` (seed 2026110101).
- `output/orbital14-binary-20261101-pilot.csv` and `.log` (seed 2026110102).
- `output/orbital14-binary-20261101-retry.csv` and `.log` (same seed, fresh
  solver with a larger budget, not checkpoint continuation).
- `output/orbital14-klein-20261101-retry.csv` and `.log` (seed 2026110101).

None of these 68 exact partitions was registered in the inspected historical
`output/orbital-sat-20261014.sqlite` ledger. That does not rule out overlaps
up to relabelling or previously explored colourings, but explains using a
fresh ledger for these pilots without losing an existing exact exclusion.

Validation commands:

```sh
python3 -m unittest discover -s scripts -p 'test_*.py'
GRAPHY_BINARY=target/release/graphy-u256 python3 -m unittest discover -s scripts -p 'test_*.py'
```

Both pass: 74 tests with the wide build; the default u128 run skips the four
wide-only integration tests. This includes an independent exhaustive search
of small binary-action families, a positive control recovering a refuter
after exception release, the n=17 model's explicit relabelling to the known
refuter, and launcher/ledger/native-SAT checks of the new n=17 partitions.
Rust code and binaries were unchanged, so there was no Rust rebuild.

## Running campaigns

Use the **same orbital ledger** when revisiting a given order. Existing
exact partition identities remain valid, including old custom partition
files if their cells are identical. Isomorphic but differently labelled
partitions are not automatically merged. The saved short-trial report in
`klein-codex.txt` is useful context but is not itself an importable certificate
of a terminal task outcome.

`--work all` includes both untried tasks and unresolved retries; it never
retries terminal family UNSAT or a candidate already refuted in that ledger.
Repeat the selectors and variable caps when resuming. A retry starts a fresh
solver, not a continuation of the old clauses. Do not run two writers against
the same ledger, or rebuild a binary used by an active campaign.

For each command below, append `--plan` first (omit the output redirects),
inspect the eligible count and budget, then run without it. The plan is also
saved in SQLite. Output paths below are new suggested run names; choose a new
suffix for subsequent attempts to avoid overwriting your terminal logs.

### n=14: deepen the six smallest Klein-four families

```sh
python3 scripts/orbital_sat.py --order 14 --candidates output/batches/all14 \
    --ledger output/orbital-binary14-20261101.sqlite \
    --preset klein --max-variables 30 --work all \
    --seconds 120 --jobs 5 --rng-seed 2026110103 \
    > output/orbital14-klein-next-1.csv 2> output/orbital14-klein-next-1.log
```

At most 144 tasks with the current 24 candidates: 4.8 worker hours, roughly
58 minutes at five jobs if every task uses its budget. This is a budget
calculation, not a prediction of success or of completion of those families.
Time limits are soft around exact matching calls, and scheduling/CPU overhead
can increase wall time. There is no justification yet for week-long retries.

The next Klein-four layer, without repeating that <=30-variable batch, is
`--preset klein --min-variables 31 --max-variables 35 --work all`, again with
120 seconds. Its 12 families give at most 288 tasks, about 1h55 at five jobs.

### Broader binary portfolio

For n=14, start with the small higher-rank families:

```sh
python3 scripts/orbital_sat.py --order 14 --candidates output/batches/all14 \
    --ledger output/orbital-binary14-20261101.sqlite \
    --preset binary --max-variables 25 --work all \
    --seconds 30 --jobs 5 --rng-seed 2026110104 \
    > output/orbital14-binary-next-1.csv 2> output/orbital14-binary-next-1.log
```

Use the plan's **remaining** task count: after the pilots this is eleven
tasks, about 66 seconds of budget at five workers (plus scheduling/overhead;
three waves of 30-second tasks can take about 90 seconds). This cap does not
overlap the n=14
Klein-four families, which start at 28 variables. A later expansion to
`--min-variables 26 --max-variables 30` adds 21 families, six of them Klein
families; unresolved Klein tasks will be retried if included.

For other orders, substitute the desired n, candidate file, and orbital
ledger, and initially use `--preset binary --max-variables 30 --seconds 30
--jobs 5 --work all`. With the current survivor counts the total budget caps
before ledger exclusions are about 4h24 at n=15 (44*60 tasks), 3h06 at n=16
(62*30 tasks), and 1h35 at n=17 (38*25 tasks). These portfolios have not all
been benchmarked locally. A five-second first pass is a reasonable way to
separate cheap exclusions from tasks that justify a larger budget.

In particular, the existing n=17 ledger can be extended directly:

```sh
python3 scripts/orbital_sat.py --binary target/release/graphy-u256 \
    --order 17 --candidates output/batches/all17 \
    --ledger output/orbital-sat17-20261101.sqlite \
    --preset binary --max-variables 30 --work all \
    --seconds 30 --jobs 5 --rng-seed 2026110105 \
    > output/orbital17-binary-next-1.csv 2> output/orbital17-binary-next-1.log
```

Status and recovery of committed outcomes:

```sh
python3 scripts/orbital_sat.py --ledger output/orbital-binary14-20261101.sqlite --status
python3 scripts/orbital_sat.py --ledger output/orbital-binary14-20261101.sqlite \
    --export-csv > output/orbital14-binary-all-attempts.csv
```

An UNSAT outcome still means only `family-unsat-unverified`, not universality
and not a separately certified SAT proof. Refutations should be independently
checked and cross-culled against the other candidates before editing survivor
lists. Existing ledger behavior does not cross-cull them automatically.

## What “no observed slowdown” referred to

The previous comparison was **old versus corrected u128 builds**, on three
deterministic 200-step n=14 counted-repair trials. All non-time CSV fields
matched; median wall times were 1.154834s and 1.094725s respectively. That was
a small check for an obvious regression from safer arithmetic and counters,
not evidence of a speedup.

It was **not** a u128-versus-U256 comparison. It does not establish that the
default u128 build can be discarded. Such a decision needs matched-work
benchmarks of the two current corrected builds across the relevant matchers,
SAT workloads, and enumeration commands, not the old pre-fix U256 backup.
