## The n=16 witnesses belong to one small construction

Read in order of the five successful lines in `output/seek-16-22f.csv`:

| ID | Host as stored, not minimum-decimal | Edges | Aut order | Pair orbits |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| H1 | 1289837151261995308861632062003576832 | 57 | 256 | 15 |
| H2 | 824611412161834803513761520815295742 | 59 | 256 | 15 |
| H3 | 977306710830422049899698733851852800 | 58 | 256 | 15 |
| H4 | 1248374899284764555899993718739444227 | 60 | 256 | 15 |
| H5 | 824611412161834803513761520815295743 | 60 | 256 | 15 |

H4 is isomorphic to the complement of H5. H2 and H5 differ by just the
edge (0,1) in the stored labeling. H1 and H3 are also one edge apart up to
isomorphism, as the following construction shows.

Let Q be the four-set {0,1,2,3}, equipped with the cycle 0-1-2-3-0.
Construct a 16-vertex graph with:

1. Six singleton vertices A_S, one for each two-subset S of Q.
2. Four pairs B_i of vertices, each pair inducing K_2. Put all four edges
   between B_i and B_j exactly when ij is a cycle edge.
3. Two more vertices x,y, each adjacent to all six A_S and no B_i.
4. Join A_S to both vertices of B_i exactly when i belongs to S.
5. Choose independently whether to include each of the three edges
   A_S--A_(Q\S), and whether to include xy. No other edges are present.

There are only 16 labeled choices in step 5. Their fixed part has 56 edges:
24 incidence edges, 16 edges between adjacent B pairs, four internal B
edges, and 12 edges from x,y to A. Up to isomorphism and complement they
contain all five witnesses:

| Witness | Edges between complementary A pairs | xy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| H1 | none | present |
| H2 | all three | absent |
| H3 | just A_{0,2}--A_{1,3} | present |
| H5 | all three | present |
| H4 | complement of H5 | — |

Every identification was checked with `labelg`, not inferred from equal
group orders or decimal proximity. All have five twin pairs and six
singleton vertices. The relevant group combines square symmetries with
independent swaps of the five pairs, of order 8*2^5=256.

Their complete cross-refutation matrix on the five eliminated candidates
was checked with the Rust matcher (1 means a refutation):

| Candidate | H1 | H2 | H3 | H4 | H5 |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 108262796953582592 | 1 | 0 | 1 | 0 | 0 |
| 108262796959906816 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| 108262796961971200 | 1 | 0 | 1 | 0 | 0 |
| 108262796964067328 | 0 | 1 | 0 | 1 | 1 |
| 108263071833585664 | 0 | 1 | 0 | 1 | 1 |

Thus two witnesses, one from each column type, suffice for these five
refutations. Equal coverage here does not imply interchangeable behavior
on other candidate sets.

The square action with just the independent x/y swap has 24 pair orbits.
Adding the four independent swaps inside B_i reduces this to 15. The latter
has only 2^15=32,768 orbital unions before edge filtering, versus 2^24 for
the weaker action. This is a targeted restriction to graphs with the
specified twins, not a claim that all graphs of the weaker action survive
this reduction. It captures the useful examples much more cheaply.
For H1, checking its entire 256-element automorphism group found that even
its best single cyclic action has 22 pair orbits.

As a bounded follow-up, all four internal B_i edges were also made optional.
Together with the other four optional edges this gives 256 labeled variants,
or 76 isomorphism classes. None refuted any of the 33 survivors in the file.
This negative result suggests varying the incidence template or twin-cell
sizes, not simply repeating these internal-edge toggles.

## The new n=14 witness extends an existing 6+6+2 pattern

For H=19045246657205181093300321, dreadnaut gives:

- 45 edges, degrees 6^8 and 7^6;
- automorphism group order eight, isomorphic to C_4 x C_2;
- 25 unordered-pair orbits;
- vertex-orbit sizes 4,4,2,2,2; only one twin pair, vertices 12 and 13.

All three of the new n=14 witnesses from September 17 and September 23
fit this template: take a graph A on six vertices, a disjoint copy B of
its complement, a 3-regular bipartite graph between A and B, and two
independent twins joined to all A and no B. Its edge count is always

    |E(A)| + (15-|E(A)|) + 18 + 12 = 45.

The September 17 examples use A=K_{3,3} and A=3K_2. The new example uses
A=C_4 disjoint union K_2. In the stored labeling A is vertices 0..5,
B is 6..11, and the twin pair is 12,13. This points to varying A and the
3-regular cross-connections beyond the previously productive cyclic
choices. The whole template is still large; candidate-directed repair or
constraint search would be preferable to an indiscriminate enumeration.

The full eight-element automorphism group was checked for possible cyclic
generators. Every cyclic construction of this graph needs at least 29 pair
orbits; minimizing cycle types are 4+4+2+2+2 and 4+4+2+2+1+1.
However, it does not intrinsically need a 29-orbit cap in the layered family.
Two copies of the two-subset layer of a four-set, with a simultaneous
four-cycle and an independent twin swap, have 25 orbits and contain H.
That exact orbit partition already appears in `layered_actions(14)`.
Consequently the cap needed depends on the action family, not merely on
the graph's full automorphism group.

An explicit identification of this action: in each six-vertex layer use
the permutation (0 3 5 2)(1 4), with the second layer shifted by six;
also allow (12 13) independently. Map its standard vertices to the stored
H labeling by

    0,2,1,4,3,5,8,6,10,9,7,11,12,13.

H pulled back along that map is constant on each of the 25 pair orbits.

## Streaming and encoding measurements

The user's two commented-out dedup lines remove the unbounded labeled-graph
cache from Python. `emitted` is now unused but remains empty, so it does not
cause the prior memory growth. The partition cache remains small. This is
a sound way to feed a separate deduplicator; no extra Python set is needed.

The external cache is still proportional to the number of distinct keys,
so the pipeline is streaming but not bounded-memory. The completed library
contains 19,338,415 lines. On its first one million already-canonical entries,
with output discarded, `/usr/bin/time` reported:

| Deduplicator | Wall seconds | Peak RSS, KiB |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| `awk '!x[$0]++'` | 1.41 | 333,208 |
| `uniqg -cq` | 1.74 | 173,568 |

These are isolated single-run measurements on unique input, not full-run
CPU/memory predictions. `uniqg -c` skips canonicalization because `labelg`
already performed it. Alternatively, raw generated graph6 can go directly
to `uniqg -q`, which canonicalizes internally. Keeping the two stages can
let canonicalization and deduplication run on separate cores.

The installed `uniqg` uses SHA-256 fingerprints rather than full graph
comparisons. That is reasonable for a heuristic library: a hypothetical
collision could lose a search opportunity, but cannot make an independently
checked refuter invalid. It should not replace exact keys in an exhaustive
search's visited-state set. See the [nauty 2.9.3 guide](https://users.cecs.anu.edu.au/~bdm/nauty/nug29.pdf),
section 24, for `uniqg` options and comparison semantics.

A small Python profile, without pipe output, was dominated by graph6
encoding. Replacing per-bit summation with a 64-entry lookup table of
bit-reversed sextets gave the same output for random graphs at every order
1..62. On 100,000 n=14 encodings the old function took 2.04 s and the trial
function 0.25 s. This is approximately 8x for the encoder, not for the whole
pipeline; nauty or output can become the next bottleneck. The production
encoder was not changed in this investigation.

Other prospective generator improvements are to enumerate cheap partitions
first (improves time to useful output, not total work), deduplicate orbit
partitions up to vertex relabeling, or exploit symmetries of the orbit
variables themselves. None can be assumed to give a large speedup without
measuring the affected duplicates. The targeted full-group/twin templates
above are a separate way to prioritize a smaller useful search space.

## The FFI prototype works and has measurable benefits

The standalone crate compiles five unchanged upstream C files and a
32-line adapter, through a small Cargo build script using `cc`. All are
compiled with `WORDSIZE=32`, `MAXN=32`, and `USE_TLS`. The caller supplies
ordinary arrays of 32 u32 adjacency rows; C constructs all nauty structures
and returns the full canonical adjacency rows. Rust can pack these into
the existing BitNum for cache storage. No Rust Graph struct, u128/u256 value,
allocation ownership, or callback crosses the initial ABI.

This addresses the data-layout concern by translating explicitly rather
than assuming nauty's bit ordering or structures match graphy's. For
mixed-order caches the key must retain n; fixed-order search caches can
keep n implicit. Nauty scratch state must be thread-local in every linked
C object, not just in the adapter. The compatibility check is called too.
See [the Cargo build-script examples](https://doc.rust-lang.org/cargo/reference/build-script-examples.html)
for the static C build/link pattern and the nauty guide's section 16 for TLS.

Validation completed:

- All 1,024 labeled five-vertex graphs gave 34 distinct keys.
- All 32,768 labeled six-vertex graphs matched `labelg` output exactly,
  giving 156 classes.
- A further 1,000 random graphs at orders 14,16,17,23,32 matched `labelg`
  exactly. Installed utilities are 2.9.3; the local source in this probe
  is 2.8.9. This agreement is an observation, not a cross-version guarantee.
- Reversal invariance, idempotence, edge-count preservation and graph6
  round trips were tested at every order 0..32.
- Eight concurrent workers performed 8,192 calls and matched serial results.

A standalone successors implementation used this key, retaining BTreeSet
membership and the all-retractions strategy, without edge-orbit pruning.
Both versions returned exactly the same graph classes:

| Stored input | Entries | Existing Rust, seconds | FFI prototype, seconds | Ratio |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `output/batches/all14` | 26 | 0.5981 | 0.0911 | 6.6x |
| `output/batches/all15` | 67 | 2.5991 | 0.2442 | 10.6x |
| `output/batches/all16` | 33 | 2.0282 | 0.1810 | 11.2x |

These are single-run whole-command measurements on these small batches,
including process startup. The prototype prints nauty-canonical graph6;
the legacy command prints minimum-decimal CSV. Only 1, 2, and 0 successors
respectively were printed, so output-format costs were small. Integration
and larger workloads still need benchmarking. The n=14 input and README
still contain the newly refuted graph; these were deliberately not edited.

On the earlier dense 1,000-graph n=14 orbital sample, 100 repetitions of
in-process nauty canonicalization took 0.476451 s total (about 4.8 microseconds
per call), while one legacy graphy pass took 3.9148 s. This large canon-only
ratio must not be substituted for a whole-seek speedup.

## Integration boundaries and remaining complexity

An existing [nauty-Traces-sys crate](https://github.com/a-maier/nauty-Traces-sys)
provides bundled nauty and TLS support. Its currently published 0.11.0
[build script](https://docs.rs/crate/nauty-Traces-sys/latest/source/build.rs)
bundles nauty 2.9.3 and uses both cc and bindgen. Bindgen adds a libclang
build dependency. That is a viable packaging option; a tiny C adapter avoids
binding the wider API but leaves us responsible for packaging/configuring
a pinned upstream source. Neither approach reimplements nauty's algorithms.

The proof of concept deliberately uses an existing configured source tree;
making a clean checkout build reliably is remaining production work, not
something this experiment has solved. A pinned, reproducible dependency is
preferable to linking arbitrary prebuilt local objects with unknown TLS or
word-size options. Tests do not eliminate the usual trusted-C-code risk.

The next production change can be narrow: add a separate canonical-key API,
then use it in successors and seek's visited-state cache. Leave `to_best`,
`is_best`, the old enumeration and human-facing minimum-decimal output
semantics alone initially. `--max` need not be removed; its uncommon path
can retain the old ordering-based implementation during the transition.
There is no conflict with keeping the old enumerator as a convenient tool.

Small additional observation: installed `geng -ql 6` already matched default
`labelg` output on all 156 generated graphs. `geng` without `-l` does not
request canonical output. Compatibility should still be tested with the
specific program versions and options used.

Future edge-orbit pruning would require obtaining automorphism generators,
but that should be a separate step. Ordinary nauty is not a replacement
for the project's non-induced subgraph matcher or its ingraph-specific
search logic. Faster canonicalization helps successors and seek; it does
not directly speed matcher-only culls, the existing labelg pipeline, or
the nauty-based enumeration dominating exhaustive proof runs.

Suggested sequence: retain the streaming behavior and optimize graph6
encoding; integrate the native key first in successors with comparison
tests; integrate it in seek; then benchmark seeded/candidate-directed repair
and the targeted refuter templates. No multi-hour search was launched here.
