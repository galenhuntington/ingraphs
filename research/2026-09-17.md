## What the new n=14 witnesses look like

Both have 45 edges, automorphism group order 24, and vertex orbits of
sizes 6, 6, and 2. They have a compact description. Write their vertices as
`A_0,...,A_5`, `B_0,...,B_5`, and `x,y`, with subscripts modulo 6. In both
graphs, x and y are nonadjacent and each is adjacent to all six A vertices
and none of the B vertices. The remaining edges are:

| Witness | Within A | Within B | A_i adjacent to B_j when j-i is in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 9385953849291249642658467 | K_{3,3}, even vs odd indices | two triangles, even and odd indices | {1,2,3} |
| 19045265114045039286486176 | opposite-pair matching, difference 3 | K_6 minus the opposite-pair matching | {3,4,5} |

Simultaneously rotating A and B is an automorphism. Rotating only one
block generally is not. This is the distinction that the independent
parts in the original generator missed. The first witness has degree
multiset `5^6 6^2 8^6`; the second has `6^8 7^6`.

## Generator changes

`scripts/orbitals.py` now computes unordered-pair orbits directly from
permutation generators. It no longer enumerates the entire group or
discards groups larger than 20,000 elements. Traversing generator images
of pairs suffices: in a finite permutation group each inverse is a
positive power, so this reaches exactly the full orbit.

Different groups with the same pair-orbit partition produce the same
unions. The script deduplicates these partitions before enumerating
unions, and prunes partial unions using their possible edge counts.

The original three positional arguments still select the layered family.
`--family cyclic` adds one permutation of every cycle type on n vertices,
up to relabelling, including full circulants and coupled rotations on
several blocks. `--family all` combines both families. The orbit cap is
configurable with `--max-orbits` (default 18). This cap and the selected
edge interval still make these heuristic libraries, not exhaustive searches.
Use `--family cyclic --cycles 6,6,2` to restrict generation to one chosen
cycle type. For n=14 this gives just 2,298 isomorphism classes in 35..45
edges and finds both new refutations from the broader 29,564-graph library.
This is a reduction identified after the broad search, not a claim that
the small family would have been the best choice in advance.

Use nauty for library deduplication:

```sh
python3 scripts/orbitals.py 14 35 45 --family cyclic --graph6 \
  | labelg -q | sort -u > output/cyclic14.g6
target/release/graphy cull 14 output/batches/all14 output/cyclic14.g6
```

Graphy's input parser already accepts graph6. Nauty's canonical labels
are different from graphy's minimal decimal labels, but this does not
affect containment. Convert only useful witnesses with `graphy canon`
when convenient. This avoids applying the slower canonicalizer to every
raw library entry.

For the first 1,000 raw n=14 graphs of cycle type 6+6+2 in 35..45 edges,
the observed wall times were 4.633 seconds for `graphy canon` and 0.0078
seconds for `labelg -q`. Recanonicalizing nauty's output with graphy gave
exactly the same 1,000 results as canonicalizing the original decimals.
These are sample timings during the research session, not a universal
performance ratio.

The n=14 cyclic library also recovers the previously elusive witness
`38450669534518018066231521`, up to complement and isomorphism, and the
September 11 witness `19045265112462908685107233`.

## Limits of an orbital-only search

An orbital-union graph for a group K has K as a subgroup of its
automorphism group. Consequently its full automorphism group has no more
pair orbits than K. The following full-group orbit counts were computed
with nauty's `dreadnaut` generators:

| Known witness | Automorphism group order | Unordered-pair orbits |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 9385956373809257868125953 | 2 | 51 |
| 19074212796290016155949665 | 8 | 22 |
| 19045246656729227906847233 | 8 | 35 |

No choice of group family with an 18-orbit cap can generate any of these
graphs. Increasing that cap to 51 would entail up to 2^51 unions for an
action. This explains part of the observed incompleteness. A broader
catalogue of symmetric actions helps, but it cannot by itself resolve
the symmetry-breaking cases at a small orbit cap.

The n=17 cloning result makes this more concrete:

| New n=17 witness | Automorphism group order | Pair orbits | Refutations assigned to it by the cull |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 83743919474059250318485221554066818208 | 8640 | 11 | 27 |
| 168817107670779710299111768675823092896 | 288 | 19 | 4 |
| 168817111969725279296181501561201497884 | 2 | 121 | 20 |

The last graph's only nonidentity automorphism swaps its two twin
vertices. Direct orbital enumeration would require at least 121 Boolean
orbit choices, yet vertex cloning found it cheaply. Assigned counts are
from first hits in the library, not a claim that the three refuted sets
are disjoint if all hosts are tested against every candidate.

`scripts/refuter_variants.py` provides complementary, bounded explorations:
edge flips, all cut switchings, arbitrary replacement of one vertex's
neighborhood, and true/false twin cloning. None of these transformations
is claimed to preserve refutation; every generated graph needs checking.
`research/seeds14.txt` contains the eight late witnesses from the notes.

To lift the productive n=16 graphs to n=17, pad each decimal encoding
with an isolated seventeenth vertex, then run the clone operation. In
particular, replacing that isolated vertex by a twin extends the original
graph by one vertex. The operation also tries replacements of the other
vertices; every output is tested. Reproduction:

```sh

  | sort -u > output/new-refuters16.txt
python3 scripts/refuter_variants.py 17 output/new-refuters16.txt --mode clone --graph6 \
  | labelg -q | sort -u > output/lift17.g6

```

## The growth rate is Theta(n log n)

Let D(n) be the maximum edge count of a universal ingraph for n, allowing
disconnected graphs as in the existing definition. The following arguments
settle its order of growth, although their constants are far from useful
for n=14.

**Upper bound.** Let F have v nonisolated vertices, m>=1 edges, and a
automorphisms. In an independent uniform red/blue coloring of K_n, each
particular copy of F is monochromatic with probability 2^(1-m). There
are (n)_v/a copies, where (n)_v=n!/(n-v)!. Universality implies that their
expected monochromatic count is at least one. Hence

    m <= 1 + log_2((n)_v/a) <= 1 + log_2(n!) = O(n log n).

This also gives a candidate-specific necessary condition, though it is
too weak to eliminate the present small candidates.

**Lower bound.** The elementary Ramsey recurrence gives
R(K_k,K_k) <= binomial(2k-2,k-1) <= 4^k. Set

    k = floor((log_2 n - 2 log_2 log_2 n)/2)

for sufficiently large n, so k~(log_2 n)/2 and r=4^k=o(n). Repeatedly
remove a monochromatic K_k while at least r vertices remain. This finds
at least t=floor((n-r)/k) pairwise vertex-disjoint monochromatic cliques.
At least ceil(t/2) have the same color. Thus the fixed graph

    F = ceil(t/2) disjoint copies of K_k

is universal for n: extra edges between the cliques can simply be omitted
because containment is not induced. Its edge count is

    ceil(t/2) * binomial(k,2) = (1/8 - o(1)) n log_2 n.

Together these show D(n)=Theta(n log n), excluding both eventually linear
and quadratic growth. The exact leading constant is not determined here.
Replacing each clique in F by a balanced complete bipartite subgraph still
gives Theta(n log n) edges, so this argument does not force extremizers to
eventually become nonbipartite. It also does not establish this lower bound
under an additional connectedness requirement.

A relevant literature connection is Chung and Erdos's (n,e)-unavoidable
graphs: graphs contained in **every** n-vertex graph with e edges. This is
a stronger requirement than being an ingraph. For e=ceil(binomial(n,2)/2),
such a graph is automatically a universal ingraph, since one color has
at least e edges. Their dense-regime Theta(n log n) result is summarized
in the introduction of Bucic, Draganic and Sudakov,
[Universal and unavoidable graphs](https://people.math.ethz.ch/~sudakovb/universal-and-unavoidable.pdf),
Combinatorics, Probability and Computing 30 (2021), 942-955. The elementary
argument above is self-contained; no novelty claim is intended.

## Why the current surviving candidates must be bipartite

If a universal ingraph F has a connected component larger than ceil(n/2),
then F must be bipartite. To see this, color two cliques of orders
floor(n/2) and ceil(n/2) red, and every cross edge blue. The large component
cannot fit in either red component, so F must occur in the blue complete
bipartite graph. Every subgraph of that graph is bipartite.

Every graph in each original batch for n=14,15,16,17 is connected and has
more than ceil(n/2) nonisolated vertices. The observed bipartiteness of
those candidates is therefore forced by this simple obstruction. What
remains unexplained is why the densest survivors have these vertex and
component sizes in the first place.

## Next search direction

For the low-symmetry gap, a candidate-directed SAT search is worth a
controlled experiment. Give each host edge a Boolean color variable.
Whenever the existing matcher finds a red copy of F, add a clause requiring
one of its edges to be blue, and symmetrically for blue copies. Every such
clause is valid for every refuter. This avoids enumerating all embeddings
up front, but may still need very many iterations. Restricting variables
to an orbital partition can make an initial experiment small; UNSAT under
that restriction proves only that the family has no refuter. Proving
universality would require the unrestricted encoding, sound symmetry
constraints, and a checked proof. No SAT performance claim or n=14
universality proof is made here.
