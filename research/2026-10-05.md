## Outcome

Implemented resumable, disjoint vertex-region scheduling in
`scripts/sat_repair.py`, using `scripts/sat_ledger.py` (SQLite, standard library).
The native additions are catalogue-only output and explicit region-file input;
the SAT formula, separation oracle and success checks are unchanged.
The complete protocol is in the README's **Resumable vertex catalogues** section.

`output/sat-regions.sqlite` is populated with all eight
`output/sat{14,15,16}-vertex3-wide*.csv` campaigns and the October 3 vertex

Arbitrary scattered-edge blocks are not conflated with vertex regions.
The ledger also recognizes prescribed masks that are exactly whole-star unions,
so the old explicit replays can resolve the corresponding catalogue entries.
Input CSVs, survivor lists, and the user's untracked files were not changed.

No new refutation was found in this session.

```sh
python3 scripts/sat_repair.py --ledger output/sat-regions.sqlite \
    --order 14 --candidates output/batches/all14 --vertices 3 --work new \

```

If costs resemble the pilot, this is roughly an hour on five workers; treat
that as a rough extrapolation, not a promise. The very pessimistic per-block
budget sum is 117.2 worker-hours, plus untimed overhead. For a bounded first
installment, change `--blocks 0` to `--blocks 512` (per candidate, **not** per
worker). Repeating the command with a fresh output filename continues to new
regions. It will not spend another pass on the completed ones.

Next, retry the remaining limited regions with e.g. `--work retry --seconds 120
--rounds 100000 --chunk-size 1`; keep `--blocks 0` and the candidate/order filters.
Increasing seconds alone may hit the independent round cap. There is no automatic
escalation and no restoration of solver learning. The ordinary new pass deliberately
does not keep retrying its own timeouts.

## The new refuter is a small S3 template

Let

```
H = 156118014097567454983522103044
F = 106450004178944
```

`research/refuter15_structure.py` independently checks that both H and its
complement avoid F, enumerates all automorphisms by exact induced matching,
and verifies the following explicit construction. It uses no external Python
graph library.

H has **52 edges**, degree multiset **6^4 7^8 8^3**, no twins, and exactly
**six automorphisms**. The group is S3: simultaneous permutations of the index
positions in four triples. In the user's least-decimal labels, align the triples
as follows (the order within each triple matters):

```
A = (1, 3, 6)       B = (2, 4, 7)
C = (9, 8, 5)       D = (12, 13, 14)
singletons: 0, 10, 11
```

The complete recipe is:

- B and C are triangles; A and D are independent.
- A–B, B–C, and C–D are index-matching edges, e.g. A[i]–B[i].
- A–C, A–D, and B–D have all edges **except** index-matching edges.
- Vertex 0 joins all of C and D; 10 joins all of A and C; 11 joins all of A and B.
- The only edge among the three singletons is 10–11.

So this is structured, not an apparently asymmetric exceptional graph. Its seven
vertex orbits have sizes 3,3,3,3,1,1,1. However, its symmetry is substantially
weaker than that of the 256-automorphism n=16 refuters.

### Why the orbital searches can miss it

The **full** automorphism group has **31 orbits on all unordered vertex pairs**:
22 of size 3, six of size 6, and three of size 1. Fifteen of the pair orbits are
red in H. Importantly, the generator's cap counts red **and blue** pair orbits,
not just the occupied ones.

No subgroup of the automorphism group can have fewer pair orbits. Therefore
**any orbital-action enumeration capped at 30 is incapable of emitting H**,
even under a relabelling or complementation.

For the cyclic family, the best automorphism has cycle type
`3,3,3,3,1,1,1`, giving **37** pair orbits. Each involution has four 2-cycles
and seven fixed points, giving 65; the identity has 105. Thus the cyclic family
needs **max-orbits at least 37**, not merely 31, and an edge window containing
52 (or 53 for the complement). The natural full-S3 action has seven layers,
beyond `orbitals.py`'s current `MAX_LAYERS = 5`.

The corresponding invariant template has 31 binary choices:
four internal-triple switches; two switches for each of six triple pairs
(matching and off-matching); twelve triple–singleton switches; and three
singleton–singleton switches. Exhausting it already means 2^31 labelled
assignments before density pruning. Merely increasing global orbital caps is
therefore not an obviously economical way to exploit the observation.

### Bounded neighbourhood checks

Generated H and every toggle of up to three **whole pair orbits** of its full
S3 action: 1 + 31 + 465 + 4,495 = **4,992 labelled graphs**. This is not a
three-edge radius: an orbit contains 1, 3, or 6 edges.

Files: `output/refuter15-20261005-orbit3.txt`, `...-orbit3-cull.csv`, and
`...-orbit3-still-refutes.csv`.

- No member refutes any of the current 60 n=15 survivors.
- Only three labelled members still refute the original F. All three are
  isomorphic to H up to complementation: **no other refuter class** was found
  for F in this symmetry-preserving neighbourhood.
- Taking all one-vertex deletions of the 497 hosts within two orbit flips gives
  7,455 labelled n=14 graphs, or **2,389 classes** up to isomorphism/complement.
  None refutes any of the current 25 n=14 survivors.

Reproduce the generator with:

```sh
python3 research/refuter15_structure.py --depth 3
python3 research/refuter15_structure.py --depth 2 --delete-one
```

These checks do **not** rule out a useful larger neighbourhood, symmetry-breaking
moves, or a different S3 template. They do temper the immediate "fertile nest"
hypothesis for small orbit-preserving moves. H can still be used as a seed;
copy count alone is not a decisive measure of seed quality.

The successful original SAT region came from the **third** seed class, with
124 monochromatic copies, not the first class with 60. The two successful
three-vertex regions had fixed-core classes absent from the first two seeds'
catalogue, and solved in about 0.017 and 0.029 seconds. That is why broader
core coverage remains the primary recommendation, rather than universally
larger per-block timeouts.
