# Incremental SAT repair: first experiments, 2026-10-02

## Outcome

**No new refuters.** Survivor lists remain 25 at n=14, 61 at n=15, and 31
at n=16; those lists and the proof scripts were not edited. The focused n=15
candidate `105898625070080` remains at four recorded copies, and the closest
n=14 candidate `208095050752` remains at two.

There are **299 frontier block outcomes**: 165 local UNSATs, 131 time limits,
and three input-constraint limits. Their summed block wall time is about
918 seconds (15.3 minutes), spread across concurrent workers. This is not a
controlled single-core speed benchmark. No completed score improved the
input pools' minima. Incomplete/capped scores are blank, not zero.

The new information is that small blocks are often **exhausted**, not merely
unsuccessfully searched. All 24/32/40-edge blocks tested around the four-copy
n=15 plateau were UNSAT. Larger blocks become substantially harder, with the
time going predominantly into SAT, not graph matching.

## Implementation and build

- Optional Cargo feature `sat`; default builds do not include the new dependency.
- `rustsat` and `rustsat-cadical` pinned to `=0.7.5`, with explicit feature
  `v2-2-1`. Tests and every experiment confirmed signature `cadical-2.2.1`.
- New `graphy ingraph-sat` command in `src/repair/sat.rs`.
- `scripts/sat_repair.py` selects existing candidate-specific CSV seeds,
  schedules independent processes, and flushes each block result. Parallel
  processes are independent; blocks within one process share solver state.
- Bounded witness collection reuses the existing copy traversal. It stops
  after a configurable number of distinct witnesses per colour. A partial
  traversal yields valid witnesses but no count or absence claim.
- Optional DIMACS snapshots, prescribed free-edge masks, failed-assumption
  variable masks, native search statistics, and SAT/oracle timing splits.

The small adapter uses RustSAT's safe API. No handwritten FFI, solver fork,
or new vendor tarball was added. The binding compiles its bundled C/C++.

The initial build found the system libclang but could not load one of its
dependencies from the Nix executable's environment. A temporary Nix shell
supplied a consistent libclang environment; no user profile was changed:

```sh
nix-shell -p rustPlatform.bindgenHook --run 'cargo build -r --features native,sat'
```

The experiments used the release build with `native,sat`; no extra CXXFLAGS
were set. `native` tunes Nauty, not the dependency's C++ flags. An optional
`CXXFLAGS=-march=native` can be supplied at build time for CaDiCaL as well.

## Exact meaning of the experiment

An edge variable is true for red and false for blue. Each full labelled copy
C contributes two permanent clauses:

```
OR(e in C) x_e
OR(e in C) NOT x_e
```

Together they require a mixed-colour copy. For a selected free-edge set B,
the values of all edges outside B are passed as **temporary assumptions**.
No block-simplified clause is ever saved for reuse with other assumptions.
All application variables are explicitly declared before solving, including
when optional BVA (`--factor`) is enabled.

The solver returns a provisional colouring. The matcher finds monochromatic
copies, their clauses are added, and the process repeats. A refuter must pass
complete absence checks in both colours and then the separate existence
matcher. Frontier outputs never received a `refuted` status in this session.

A local UNSAT excludes every assignment of the selected B, with the other
edges fixed. It does **not** search every graph at edit distance <= |B|.
Different blocks overlap and are not independent uniform graph samples.
Even 16 UNSAT 40-edge blocks are not a radius-40 exclusion or a proof of
universality. The whole-space UNSAT status is deliberately named
`unrestricted-unsat-unverified`: this experimental tool does not generate
checked universality certificates.

`best_copies` is the minimum among fully counted hosts, not an optimization
objective for SAT. Intermediate SAT models can have many uncollected copies;
a flat recorded minimum is not, by itself, evidence that SAT made no progress.

## Positive and deliberately imperfect controls

The three known refuters were:

| n | F | Known refuter H |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 14 | 35598312519616 | 19045246657205181093300321 |
| 15 | 106036055700480 | 156271735616440115570207449267 |
| 16 | 252448629196748800 | 5152046751497651372891542837628672 |

1. **One-edge damage:** seed H xor 1, directed 32-edge blocks, batch=64,
   three seconds, root=`2026100207`. Each recovered H in its first block,
   taking 0.023, 0.027 and 0.052 seconds. Files:
   `sat-control{14,15,16}-20261002-oneflip.csv`.
2. **Sixteen-edge damage with a known feasible block:** seed H xor 65535,
   `--free-edges 65535 --blocks 1 --seconds 5 --rng-seed 2026100208`.
   Each recovered H at distance **16**, taking 0.007, 0.009 and 0.009 seconds.
   Files: `sat-control{14,15,16}-20261002-prescribed.csv`.
3. **Uninformed larger blocks:** n=14/n=15 seeds H xor 255, n=16 seed H xor 7;
   eight directed 40-edge blocks, three seconds, root=`2026100206`.
   None recovered a refuter: 18 block UNSATs and six timeouts. Critically,
   **none of these 24 blocks contained every edge of the known correction**.
   This is not evidence that SAT cannot make an eight- or three-edge repair.
   Files: `sat-control{14,15,16}-20261002.csv`.

All six positive outputs passed `research/verify_refutations.py`, whose
independent Python search does not use the Rust traversal or symmetry pruning.
They are rediscoveries of three known graphs, not six new refutations.
Cross-checking them against all current survivors at their respective orders
also yielded no additional refutations.

The controls demonstrate why block selection matters: a 16-edge correction
can be easy when its entire support is free, while a much larger block may
exclude that correction. They do not establish how far a new refuter is from
any surviving candidate's current seeds.

## Tests

- 42 Rust tests with `sat` passed in both debug and release builds (the release
  build also enabled `native`).
- All five new SAT tests also passed with `--no-default-features --features sat,u64`.
- 36 existing tests passed with `--no-default-features --features u64` (no SAT).
- 29 Python tests passed after a SAT-enabled release build, including five new
  CLI tests. They check independent copy counts/refuters, prescribed blocks,
  candidate-specific parallel scheduling, and every assignment of saved small
  DIMACS formulas, including validity of the encoded copy masks and fixed units.
  The SIGTERM test also verifies that the helper reaps its active solver child.
- Rust SAT tests include changing assumptions with a persistent solver,
  negative-literal model values, added clauses after SAT/UNSAT, BVA-enabled
  small exhaustive comparisons, interrupted oracle collection, and the
  triangle controls at n=5 (SAT) and n=6 (UNSAT).

Neither large-instance UNSAT proofs nor an independent full-size proof checker
were implemented. The large local exclusions have the trust boundary of the
tested encoding, copy oracle, binding and solver.

## Suggested next run

I would try a **short, bounded** longer-time comparison before any overnight
campaign. Increasing the budget of an already exhausted block is pointless;
48-edge n=14 blocks were still unresolved at ten seconds. This command gives
them 30 seconds, keeps four seed classes where available, and uses more streams:

```sh
python3 scripts/sat_repair.py research/repair14-seeds-2026-09-28.csv \
    --max-score 6 --keep 4 --restarts 8 --jobs 5 \
    --blocks 4 --block-size 48 --seconds 30 --batch 8 \
    --max-constraints 500000 --rng-seed 2026100210 \
    > output/sat14-long.csv 2> output/sat14-long.log
```

This selects three candidates: at most 96 blocks / 48 summed block minutes,
roughly ten minutes with five workers, plus setup/overhead. Early exhaustion
or input caps shorten it. The cap bounds input copy masks, **not total memory**;
the native solver also retains internally learned clauses. Completed rows are
flushed even if a later task fails.

Watch the **status distribution**, SAT calls and SAT/oracle time, not just
the recorded minimum Q. If these remain overwhelmingly timed out, longer runs
alone have not been justified. The next algorithmic comparison should be
structured blocks (for example whole vertex neighbourhoods) or expansion using
failed-assumption cores, rather than only increasing uniformly filled blocks.
Those strategies, and engine-option comparisons such as `--factor`, remain
unbenchmarked here. The prescribed-mask interface makes such experiments
possible without changing the solver integration.
