## What the graph looks like

This is a split graph: an independent seven-set and a seven-clique. In the
reported labels, write

- independent side: A=(0,1,2), B=(3,4,5), x=12;
- clique side: C=(6,7,8), D=(9,10,11), y=13.

The cross-adjacency matrix, in block sizes 3+3+1, is

\[
M=\begin{pmatrix}
J_3-I_3 & I_3 & \mathbf 1_3\\
I_3 & J_3-I_3 & \mathbf 0_3\\
\mathbf 1_3^T & \mathbf 0_3^T & 0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Thus there are 21 clique edges and 24 cross-edges. The degree multiset is
3^4, 4^3, 9^4, 10^3; there are no twins. This is substantially nonregular,
despite having almost half of all possible edges.

Its full automorphism group is the simultaneous S3 on the four triples,
of order six. There are 25 pair-orbits under the full group, but **at least
31 under any cyclic subgroup** (checked over all six automorphisms).
Consequently cyclic orbital generation capped at 30 could not emit it.
The natural layered description has six vertex layers, exceeding the old
generator's five-layer limit. The new SAT search does not use that limit.

This suggests balanced split graphs as another useful structural direction;
it does not establish that remaining refuters have this form. The current
implementation does not specially enforce split graphs: this structure was
discovered within the larger S3 family.

## Encoding and implementation

`graphy ingraph-orbital-sat N F --partition FILE` accepts an arbitrary partition
of all unordered vertex pairs into nonempty disjoint cells. The partition is
validated and its cells sorted. One Boolean determines the colour of each
cell, and the full host is decoded before calling the graph oracle.

If a labelled copy of F uses the variable set S, add

\[
\bigvee_{i\in S}x_i,\qquad \bigvee_{i\in S}\neg x_i.
\]

Repeated occurrences of a variable are deduplicated, not cancelled. These
clauses exclude a monochromatic copy in either colour. A one-variable copy
correctly yields contradictory units, excluding that whole family. Full graph
copy masks and projected variable masks are kept distinct; solver state never
crosses partitions. This is the existing incremental separation idea with a
new variable interpretation, not a new SAT backend.

Controlled exceptions refine the partition: every pair incident to a chosen
vertex becomes an independent variable. Remaining pieces of the old cells
remain tied. This enlarges the family; it fixes no colours, and the resulting
graph can be asymmetric. An UNSAT result for a smaller family does not exclude
its relaxed versions.

`family-unsat-unverified` is a solver conclusion about only the specified
family. It is neither universality nor a separately verified UNSAT certificate.
Optional CNF dumps include the edge-to-variable map and a full-copy witness
for every projected constraint, making the encoding auditable.

Files added:

- `src/repair/sat/orbital.rs`: native search, partition validation, diagnostics,
  CNF/witness dumps, exhaustive small tests;
- `scripts/orbital_families.py`: aligned S_k/C_k/D_k actions, cyclic actions,
  exception refinement, exact partition identities;
- `scripts/orbital_sat.py`: independent-process launcher with its own SQLite
  ledger, saved plans, stable streams, durable outcomes, resume, and export;
- `scripts/test_orbital_sat.py`: independent CNF and lifecycle tests.

The old vertex-region code/ledger is not changed. Its exclusions do not imply
these family exclusions. The new ledger refuses the old schema. It merges
identical partitions, not arbitrary isomorphic/overlapping families; in the
cyclic preset, fixed points are placed last to align the obvious C_k overlaps
with the aligned-layer catalogues.

## Bounded campaigns

The user's four five-vertex CSVs contain 728 completed attempts: 724 timeouts
and four block-UNSAT results, about 45.3 summed worker-hours. This excludes
aborted in-flight work. They reinforce the motivation for changing the family,
not simply increasing free-vertex counts.

The first orbital n=14 screen used the default triples portfolio: nine exact
partitions after the default 64-variable cap, with three seconds per task and
three jobs. Of 225 registered tasks, 220 completed: one refutation, seven
family-UNSAT results and 212 timeouts. Five queued tasks for the refuted
candidate were skipped. It took 221.9 seconds wall time.

A 30-second, two-job retry of `s3:4` and `s3:3` completed 41 tasks, yielding
31 further family exclusions and ten timeouts. Combining both runs:

- `s3:4`: all 25 input candidates resolved — the new refutation and 24 UNSATs;
- `s3:3`: 14 of the 24 survivors excluded, ten unresolved.

These are useful negative results to retain, not reasons to re-run the strict
winning family indefinitely. The lower symmetry C3 families, additional
singletons, and arbitrary-neighbourhood exceptions remain relevant.

The n=14 cyclic follow-up selected 31..34 variables, just above the user's
earlier orbital-generation cap. There are nine cycle types in this interval;
the four-triple C3 partition was already registered, leaving eight new families
for the 24 survivors. These 192 tasks at three seconds each produced 124 family
exclusions and 68 timeouts, no refutations, in 184.1 seconds wall time.

The n=16 triples screen completed 248 tasks at three seconds each, all timeouts,

## Suggested continuation

Start with four moderately relaxed n=14 families, rather than spending more
time on the fully resolved `s3:4` family or immediately freeing many more
vertices. This selects **82 eligible tasks**, at most 82 worker-minutes plus
overhead, approximately **16–20 minutes wall time on five workers** if the
machine is otherwise available. Early exclusions/hits can shorten it; this is
a budget estimate, not a speed or success guarantee.

```sh
python3 scripts/orbital_sat.py --order 14 --candidates output/batches/all14 \
    --ledger output/orbital-sat-20261014.sqlite \
    --template c3:4 --template s3:3 \
    --template s3:4/0 --template s3:4/fixed \
    --work all --seconds 60 --jobs 5 --rng-seed 2026101402 \
    > output/orbital14-focused-next.csv 2> output/orbital14-focused-next.log
```

The four families have 31, 34, 38, and 41 variables. They respectively drop
reflections, turn one triple into singletons, release one body vertex, and
release both singleton neighbourhoods. The ledger already knows the newly
refuted candidate, so leaving it in `all14` does not schedule it again.
Append `--plan` to preview/save the plan without launching SAT. Use a fresh
output name each time.

If this is mainly timeouts, a focused `--work retry --seconds 300` pass on
interesting families is reasonable; it does not resume the solver's internal
state. Alternatively change the templates rather than indefinitely increasing
time. A concrete broader family screen is:

```sh
python3 scripts/orbital_sat.py --order 14 --candidates output/batches/all14 \
    --ledger output/orbital-sat-20261014.sqlite \
    --preset cyclic --min-variables 31 --max-variables 40 \
    --work all --seconds 30 --jobs 5 --rng-seed 2026101403 --plan
```

Remove `--plan` and redirect to new output files to run. This adds the untried
35..40-variable cyclic actions and retries unresolved ones below that range;
recorded family exclusions are skipped. The full range contains 20 exact
partitions, before selecting candidates and skipping resolved tasks. This is
targeted expansion beyond the old orbit cap, not random template sampling.

python3 scripts/orbital_sat.py --order 15 --candidates output/batches/all15 \

from these short runs that the latter two caps need increasing. None hit
